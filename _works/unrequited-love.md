My grandmother used to tell me stories about her youth. I remember she had such refined ways of describing things. I was eighteen years old when I would just go into her chalet house every Saturday and ask for stories that would eventually become pieces of advice about love. I can never forget how she'd just sit there by the fireplace as I sipped hot beverages on the couch with her, laughing about random things such as the imagery of a punched kangaroo. But as soon as she started reminiscing, she'd extend the breadth of her arms toward the fireplace and close her eyes. I once asked her why she did that, and she answered with a smile, still keeping her eyes closed and her hands extended.

"The warmth given by this fire to my hands is the closest thing that resembles my piquant youth."

She once told me a story about an unrequited love of a young boy. A story that I could hardly unravel until today.

I remember she said, "He, well, he creates poetries out of things, but her, she was, for him, poetry herself. The moment they crossed eyes, the peak of floating clouds and the depths of flowing seas closed down on him; like skydiving and drastically getting pulled into the abyss, like seeing crystalized coelenterates under the sea and swimming to get closer, he had never felt his heart beat so fast."

"Did he fall in love, Grandmum?" I asked.

She giggled. "Love? No, no—That, right there, my dear girl, is a revered endeavor."

"But, Grandmum, if I feel that toward a guy, would that mean that I like him?"

It was nothing but a question from an adolescent girl trying to find answers, but she replied, "My sweet child, we have different endeavors. You may find yourself in the full extent of your hatred toward someone and, in a second, not even realize that you like him."

I remember so vividly when she said that. It gave me a sense of discomfort because what if I had liked some of the guys I used to hate, and I was just completely oblivious to the obvious stuff!?

From then on, my grandmother would always tell me little things about the story, fragments from which I could learn a thing or two. Like how friends who are unable to listen aren't friends, but mere acquaintances in your circle. Like how the demeanors, mindsets, and feelings of people are not solely their fault. That we are all made up of our childhood, experiences, and environment. Things that are so simple yet are often forgotten.

My grandmother was such a sweet lady; someone like her isn't hard to love. Now, Grandpop, Richard Reyes, and Grandmum, Rosalinda Garcia, are together in heaven. That, I'm happy about.

She had died. I'm now a twenty-one-year-old college student, but the idea of her and her stories will continue to be with me.

Collecting all of my grandparents' stuff—

"This is the first time I've ever seen this. Although I've never gone through Grandpop and Grandmum's stuff for two years, still, what is this?"

I was drawn to a notebook that seemed to contain entries of literary pieces written by my grandpop for an old lover named Liz. I opened it. The first page was entitled "Liz," and I read it.

It reads:

*She's like lilac in the midst of April through June. Her hues are those that are seen only by resting eyelashes; she has a voice heard only by listening ears. I wonder how her love feels like. Does it taste like a hot bowl of soup on an early, breezy, cold morning? Is it an imagery of drizzles in the aisle of trees in a forest? Oh, how sweet would it be to perceive every day those cool, winter-colored lips that can create new constellations at their behest. Your tan skin looks like it was gently caressed by a seamstress angel of bodies—everything suits you.*

**End of excerpt.**

Just like that, it had brought me into my grandfather's youth. I continued to read.

It reads:

*That's odd. Someone's sitting in the spot where I've been sitting in the cafeteria. I specifically chose that spot because it rests in the corner, and no one usually notices me there. Perhaps she sat there for the same reason.*

*But still, I wanna ask.*

"Excuse me, if you don't mind me asking—"

*Her hair would've looked better had it been tied up in a ponytail or something.*

"Yes?"

She turned to see me.

*Wait, she's actually so pretty. I've been here in this school for months, and I thought it was impossible to see a girl with a glowing face—*

"Eyes that have reflections that resemble the Andromeda, Whirlpool, and Cartwheel galaxies gathered in the surrounds of her iris. Wow, that face is perfectly molded; it's as if she came out of God's breath itself. Oh, thank the deities of faith and seats. She sat here, and I... just had... an engrossing, gross soliloquy—Pardon, I didn't mean to say those things out loud."

I giggled in mortification.

"Yeah. Don't mind it," she answered.

Had she told me to die right then and there, I probably would've. Her voice was majestic. It was a voice that was able to fluctuate the pillars of the highest kingdom ever made.

She was writing a consolidated paper for one of her subjects. Her byline read, "Elizabeth Perez."

After I sat beside her, she left along with her work.

I thought I'd never see her again, as she would be annoyed by my constantly feminine, assertive character. But every day, on that bench, sat that same girl, Elizabeth Perez. I would sit there beside her, and I would perform poetries as I rested my hand on top of a hot cappuccino to warm my hand and calm me down.

She never really showed interest in me. Not even once had I seen her show even the slightest admiration for me. But she would ask random things whenever she got stuck with her paperwork.

One time, she asked me, "Why do you do that?"

As vacant as her mind was, she turned to me, her honey-maple-colored hair falling beside her face.

"Do what?" I asked, closing my eyes as I rested my hand on top of a hot cappuccino.

"Do that, closing your eyes and putting your hand there."

I answered, "Well, you know, I've always felt like my youth, as it is now, feels like a warm embrace for myself. This resembles my youth. When you're feeling like you're rushing things, like you're at a pace that you cannot keep up with, try doing this. It will remind you that even hot coffee cappuccino like this has to warm up for a little bit before you taste its beauty."

She added an annoyed and annoying remark.

"You're really good with simple things."

She then left and stopped doing her paperwork. I wound up leaving as well and just got back to my own business.

I could never get her out of my head.

Elizabeth Perez, you are something. If you just let yourself perceive and appreciate me, we can be something. No, no, there's no way this is love. You're just my revered endeavor. But if I ever see your darkness, if I see tears fall from your eyes, I would love you. And not just in this time and age; I would love you for the rest of time. I will make an epoch of our love. Just please, see me.

As I was walking to school, I saw the sunlight focusing on her like spotlights staring at the main character. She was as bright as the blue sky reflecting on oceans. She was sitting under that tree of green leaves, above the green, dreamy grass. I could see that she did not make me create poetries. She was poetry.

She had her papers stacked under a book, and her hands were on top of a hot coffee cappuccino, her eyes closed. I rushed toward her, as I knew she would not be able to see me.

"Eliz! What's up?" I asked, trying to break the ice.

She spoke with her voice slightly cracking, staring right into my eyes.

"I did it."

At that moment, I thought I'd get eaten like a human baby in the midst of growing tall grass. I felt her fear.

"Did what?" I asked seriously.

I could feel her emotions bursting out as her tears fell down her face.

"I stepped back for a while, made sure I'm rested, because I felt like I was doing things in a rush. But no matter how hard I try to put my hands on top of this hot, tasteful cappuccino, I'm still as miserable! You lied! You're—I don't like you!"

It's ironic how I told myself that once I saw her cry, I'd love her, but that very moment was the same moment when she also said she didn't like me.

"Look, Liz, I'm sorry. I want you to understand that there are times when you will struggle, and it will be hard to stand back up alone. If you could just ask people to help you, open up a little bit, then maybe you'll feel better. I'm sorry that I'm not the one who is able to do that. I love you. I can feel the chills run down my spine."

She looked at me, and the moment we crossed eyes, I felt the peak of floating clouds and the depths of flowing seas close down on me; like skydiving and drastically getting pulled into the abyss. Like seeing crystalized coelenterates under the sea and swimming to get closer, I had never felt my heart beat so fast.

From that moment, I knew I'd never get her to love me.

"I couldn't figure out how people know if they're in love and when they feel that love for someone. But when I see you every day, when I want to see you at that same spot where we sit, the happiness it gives me, the way you set colors into my world ablaze... I'm smart. I know more than dozens of words, and this—the only thing I know that can explain this—is love.

She ran away, with tears fertilizing the soil. She held her papers harshly, as if she were going to throw punches. But every day, she was still right there in the cafeteria, and every day, I would fall in love with the way her honey-maple hair, tied in a ponytail, swayed. Her ever-so-soft, constantly changing-color lips, her tan skin that separated her from the rest of the lot.

I admired her from afar until we went our separate ways.

I have lived my life for four years, genuinely unable to forget.

Elizabeth Perez, you are really something.

Today, I decided to look for her. I don't know to what great lengths I'd go to find her, but I will. I want to hear her annoying remarks. I want to see her curse, both at the void and at me. I want her to feel how she used to ignore me. The odds of her having a significant other are stacked against me, but I have already let her slip through my fingers once when I didn't pursue her. I can't let it happen now.

I looked at our yearbook, one that I hadn't looked at ever since I got it. I searched for her, a year older than me in both age and college level. So much prettier, I couldn't miss her.

"Perez, Perez, where is Elizabeth Perez—"

I flipped through the pages, skipping every other name to get to Perez, but this—this was not her.

I sighed. I pondered as I saw her byline. All these years, I hadn't been corrected by her, not even once. Was I calling her the wrong name?

I giggled in despair and leaned back in my seat, as I thought I would never see her again. But the zephyr blew and flipped through the pages—I could see the sunlight focusing on her, like a theatrical play where she was speaking.

I saw her...

Rosalinda Garcia.

Her hair was still as sweet as I remembered it, her lips still as red, or winter-colored, as they used to be. Wow, her face looked like it was made out of God's breath. Even in photos, you look beautiful.

I will search for her.

"Wait, is this Grandmum writing the next excerpts?"

Update.

After this, he had found me—We've been together for five years now.

Richard's really good with simple things. It's funny how he thought it was annoying that I said that. But for me, it's one of his greatest traits. He never forgets about these simple things that make life so much greater.

He had never known, but I wasn't really a poem-type of girl. For him to say that I am poetry itself—it still makes my world fleet by so much faster.

When he said I was beautiful without a ponytail and without makeup, I realized how he really meant it through his eyes. I can't say it in a way he would, but his eyes and words were truthful.

I hadn't forgotten him, Richard Reyes, either, even after four years. I just could not.

I couldn't tell him my real name. It was just very funny to hear him say the wrong name every time. Eliz was actually a groupmate back when we were in college. I always just wrote their names before mine.

I know that, until now, he still thinks that I ran away in that moment I cried because I said I didn't like him.

The truth is, I ran away because I said things I didn't mean. And in spite of it, he was still able to be truthful and tell me his true feelings. I felt guilty, but every day, in that same spot, I still waited for him, with hot coffee cappuccino beside me, hoping that one day I'd hear his voice shouting, "Liz!"

I love you, Richard Reyes.

I love you, Rosalinda Garcia.

End of excerpt.

So that is why my Grandmum always puts her hands by the fireplace. She got it from Grandpop, and the refinement of her words is also an influence from him. They've been together for a very long time. I wonder when I could have that kind of love as well."
